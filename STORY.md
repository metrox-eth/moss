# The story so far

*Six moments. This page is written by Laurent, in his words; the dates and facts below are the frame, the lines in italics are placeholders for him.*

## 15 September 2026, a video before a robot

A 40-second concept video: a tracked rover with a bin and an arm, picking a can off the ground. Target cost said out loud: five to seven hundred dollars plus a computer. Nothing printed yet.

*What it felt like to post it, and why this robot and not another.*

## 16–19 September, the print set

Two printers for three days. The bin and a track the first evening, the hull in black PETG on the 18th. By the 19th, 113 parts on the bench, shafts and bearings in bags next to them. We forgot to take the wide shot and stitched a phone pan instead.

*The first part in hand, the first one that did not fit.*

## 20 September, the motors turn

Both motors on the bench, encoders counting, driven by the ESP32 that would drive them on the rover. Same evening, the electronics went into the hull and the Jetson booted from the battery.

*The moment the counts climbed.*

## 22 September, first drive

Tracks on, the rover moving and turning on the floor under its own motors, still tethered. The tracks had slack: one tensioner per side is not enough. That went into the next revision the same day.

*What you did right after.*

## 26 September, the arm

A standard SO-101 bolted on, gripper included, with a camera in the hand. Folded over the bin, it looks like the video. A reader looked at the photo and said the shoulder looked weak. He was right, and it became an issue.

*What a stranger seeing it taught you.*

## 1–3 October, the files are close

V0.5, redrawn on the real robot, is the release candidate for the CAD. V0.6 makes the hardware modular: Pi or Jetson, RealSense or Gemini, same chassis. Kits and a build guide are coming with Tnkr; the waitlist opened. The files come out after one test: a full battery emptied over 3 km on the beach, tracks still on, no bolt lost.

*What the beach test means to you.*
